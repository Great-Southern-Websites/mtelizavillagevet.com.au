Cancer treatment is not one-size-fits-all. At Mt Eliza Village Vet, we provide chemotherapy for selected dogs and cats, with a strong focus on comfort, quality of life and supporting families through each stage of treatment.

## Specialist-guided treatment, delivered locally

We work with Veterinary Oncology Consultants, whose board-certified veterinary oncologists review each patient's diagnosis, test results and medical history.

They provide an individually tailored treatment plan, which may include recommendations for further testing, chemotherapy protocols, monitoring and supportive care. Where appropriate, treatment can then be administered by our veterinary team at Mt Eliza Village Vet, allowing your pet to remain with a familiar local clinic.

Some patients may still require referral for specialist procedures, advanced imaging, surgery or radiation therapy.

## Chemotherapy and quality of life

Veterinary chemotherapy is generally designed to control cancer while maintaining a good quality of life. Although side effects can occur, protocols are selected carefully and many pets continue to enjoy their usual activities during treatment.

Before each treatment, your pet will be examined and will usually require a blood test. Treatment may be delayed or adjusted if your pet is unwell or their blood results indicate that additional recovery time is needed.

## Cancers that may benefit from chemotherapy

Chemotherapy may form part of the treatment plan for:

- Lymphoma and leukaemia
- Mast cell tumours
- Multiple myeloma
- Osteosarcoma
- Haemangiosarcoma
- Histiocytic sarcoma
- Bladder and urinary tract cancers
- Selected mammary, gastrointestinal and soft-tissue tumours

Whether chemotherapy is suitable depends on the tumour type, its stage and your pet's overall health.

## Safe treatment practices

Chemotherapy medications require careful preparation, administration and disposal. Our safety measures include:

- PhaSeal&trade; closed-system drug-transfer devices
- Chemotherapy-specific personal protective equipment
- Dedicated cytotoxic waste and disposal systems
- Home-care instructions for safely handling your pet's bodily waste after treatment

## Discussing your pet's options

If your pet has been diagnosed with cancer, we can help you understand the available options and decide what is right for your pet and family. This may include chemotherapy, surgery, specialist referral or comfort-focused palliative care.
