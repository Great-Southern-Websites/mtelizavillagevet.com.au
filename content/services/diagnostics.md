Our pets can't tell us when they feel unwell, so we use a range of tools and techniques to diagnose sickness in our companion animals:

- Routine, monitoring and critical bloodwork
- Urinalysis
- Cytology and histopathology
- Diagnostic imaging — X-rays, dental X-rays, ultrasound and echocardiography

## In-house bloodwork

A blood profile is usually the first tool we use when investigating illness. We evaluate red and white blood cells, organ function, glucose, electrolytes, protein and cholesterol — often with results the same day. Most pets barely notice the sample being taken, and light sedation is available for anxious patients.

## Urinalysis

Urine is "liquid gold" for diagnostics: it tells us a lot about systemic health and kidney and bladder function. We assess concentration and colour, and check for protein, blood, cells, crystals and bacteria. If you're collecting a sample at home, use a clean container and bring it in promptly — refrigerated samples should be no more than 24 hours old.

## Cytology and histopathology

For lumps and masses we use two sampling techniques: **fine needle aspiration (FNA)** to identify cell types, and **biopsy** (punch or excisional) for detailed examination by a pathologist. Biopsy results typically take one to two weeks.

## Diagnostic imaging

- **X-rays** evaluate bones and soft tissue structures
- **Ultrasound** assesses soft tissue in real time
- **Echocardiography** examines cardiac structure and blood flow — Dr Libby has a special interest in hearts and offers basic echocardiography, particularly for dogs with mitral valve disease
